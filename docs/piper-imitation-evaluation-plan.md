# First learned PiPER placement benchmark

## Claim and scope

This benchmark tests whether behavior cloning of an **authored synthetic PiPER teacher** can learn a bounded rigid-block pick-and-place skill and execute it from unseen starting positions with the same two trained goals. The demonstrations are simulator state/action traces produced by `PiperTaskPolicy`; they are not human actions, physical robot demonstrations, or video-derived actions. The result can establish a small simulation imitation-learning result only. It does not establish physical transfer, shoe manipulation, camera perception, or general-purpose skill learning.

The initial MLP candidates use 43 privileged simulator features: joint positions/velocities, gripper positions, TCP pose, block pose/velocity, goal, contact/support measurements and elapsed episode time. The stored labels are six normalized joint-target deltas plus a jaw target; the absolute-target candidate reconstructs native targets from those causal pairs. Neither candidate may use teacher phase/ticks, outcome latches, future states, demonstration identity or action labels as inputs.

Development may select a conditional feedforward movement primitive instead. Its learned references use only reset object XY, the known goal and elapsed time; live q supplies generic command limiting. Spatial polynomial and Gaussian time coefficients are fitted from the same labels, without teacher phase alignment, event-based time warping or demonstration lookup at inference. This narrower candidate does not learn contact feedback or recovery. Model-family and capacity decisions use development cases only and remain recorded with failed candidates. Freeze the actual selected feature/action contract, basis, weights, limits and implementation before confirmation.

The current environment defines a fixed 20 ms action boundary, six radian targets and a jaw target in metres. Its authored policy and pose-to-joint mapping can generate labels. The native success contract requires valid physics, sustained bilateral table-free lift, retained carry to a supported destination, an actual open-jaw release, settled destination dwell, and tool retreat. Preserve these native gates as independent outcome evidence; never train on their latches.

## Bounded split and frozen schedule

Use grouped starting positions with the same two declared goals in each split: 16 training starts arranged on a 4×4 grid (32 teacher attempts), 4 new dev starts (8 attempts), and 6 final new held-out starts (12 attempts). Keep the two goal coordinates common across splits. This deliberately measures interpolation to unseen starts with known goals; it does not test goal generalization. Assign every episode for a starting position to its one split so repeats or alternate goals cannot leak across splits. Freeze the literal coordinates, episode seeds, task constants, teacher version, and asset/model hashes before generating training demonstrations. Choose positions within the environment's bounded workspace and at least 75 mm from each assigned goal, and publish the complete schedule rather than only a random seed.

Keep all 32 scheduled teacher attempts in the report. A failed, invalid, or exceptional teacher episode remains counted with its reason and trace where available. For the initial behavior-cloning fit, use only valid successful teacher episodes; report the resulting usable episode/frame counts and exclusions by reason. The protocol sets no additional minimum success threshold: successful episodes among the fixed 32 attempts are included, and no replacement attempts are generated after seeing outcomes.

Fit preprocessing and model parameters only on train episodes. Select architecture, regularization, action scaling, stopping rule, and any threshold on dev only; log the dev selection procedure. Do not generate or read held-out demonstrations or outcomes until the dev procedure, checkpoint, inference code, feature/action schema, protocol, and hashes are frozen. Then run held-out once, with a fresh environment reset for every scheduled policy/control/scene pairing and no code or threshold edits based on those outcomes. If a protocol or implementation fix is required, retain the incomplete evidence and create a newly versioned protocol.

## Evaluation arms and reporting

Run the learned policy, the authored teacher, and three negative controls on the same frozen held-out scenes and seeds: (1) a same-recipe learner with complete seven-dimensional action labels permuted across the full training corpus using seed 19217; (2) the learned arm policy with the jaw held fully open; and (3) a no-op policy holding the reset arm targets with the jaw open. The frame-wise permutation is a weak temporal negative control; it does not preserve entire trajectories, replace a nearest-demonstration baseline or establish the value of start conditioning. Keep every training failure, invalid reset, inference error, timeout and missing output in its denominator. Do not retry based on results. Report attempted, valid, successful, failed/invalid and excluded-teacher counts separately, plus each row's reason.

The primary endpoint uses native PiPER outcome gates computed every 1 ms during execution and an external declared goal. Also report terminal XY error, lift and lift dwell, bilateral contact, retention loss, unexpected-contact ticks, release/open-jaw state, settled dwell and retreat clearance. `piper_imitation_score.score_episode` validates complete 20 ms traces and recomputes fixed-reference terminal and sustained-lift predicates alongside the recorded native gates. Its inputs are simulator-authored records, not authenticated physical sensors or an independent rerun of physics. Report per-scene outcomes and numerators/denominators. Score teacher and controls against the same scene-specific goal, and preserve full traces, requests and model/protocol/asset hashes.

`video_goal_benchmark.score_reference` remains unchanged for historical compatibility. It compares trajectories against a fixed external reference and gates on manipulation diagnostics, but its terminal dwell is calculated as `last_timestamp - streak_start`; it does not validate monotonic timestamps, expected sample cadence, or gaps between samples. A long gap can therefore look like continuous dwell. The new scorer rejects timestamp gaps/reordering, but the native PiPER `_outcome_tick` evaluates dwell at every physics tick and is the stronger primary gate. The scorer still consumes recorded native metrics; it does not replay physics to authenticate them.

## Anti-shortcut checks and release threshold

Before interpreting held-out results, audit the feature builder by inspecting the exact serialized inference input and proving it contains no teacher phase, future data, hidden outcome latches, or task labels beyond the declared goal and simulator state. Verify that model outputs alone drive every learned-policy action; no authored-policy fallback, teacher action tape, success-triggered reset, post-reset pose write, or outcome-based retry is allowed. Compare held-out start/goal coordinates with all demonstration coordinates and archive this split-overlap check. Confirm identical initial scene/reset conditions for paired arms and that the scorer's reference is read from the frozen protocol, not the policy output.

Describe the learned skill as demonstrated only if the frozen model completes at least one declared held-out case and its overall held-out success fraction is above both the shuffled-label and open-jaw/no-op controls under the same scoring rule. Regardless of outcome, publish every planned attempt and its denominator; a small schedule gives preliminary evidence, not a reliability estimate. If the teacher does not complete training demonstrations or the learned policy does not beat controls, publish that result without claiming a learned reusable skill.
